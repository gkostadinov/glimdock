# Assembly of the current r45 parts

## Hardware

| Connection | Quantity | Hardware |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Display to carrier rails | 4 | M2.5 × 6 countersunk machine screws |
| Carrier rails to front | 2 | M3 × 12 countersunk screws and captive M3 nuts |
| Rear shell to front | 4 | M3 × 12 countersunk screws and M3 heat-set inserts |

Check the actual insert dimensions against the printed pilot. Install inserts
with electronics removed. Do not use pointed coarse-thread screws in the
display's metal posts. Do not add washers beneath a countersunk bearing seat.

## Button, display and carrier

1. Clean the print and remove brim/support material from fitting surfaces.
   Carrier supports belong only in accessible receiver recesses; the flat
   factory-post pads and countersunk seats must be clear.
2. With electronics removed, dry-fit the one-piece BAT_PWR button. Its broad
   finger pad occupies the housing opening; its internal actuator faces the
   board switch. Bond only the fixed inside-wall end tabs. Keep glue away from
   the spring arms, moving pad and switch. Refit the module and confirm a free
   click and complete return before closing the housing.
3. Seat the display against its glass seat. It must lie without pressure on
   the PCB, glass, ribbon or USB socket. Put the two carrier-anchor M3 nuts in
   their front pockets before installing the rails.
4. Rail names follow the screen/front view: LEFT appears on your right when
   looking into the open rear. Keep the broad flat rail face toward the
   display and the raised M3 tower toward the open rear.
5. Start either rail **10 mm inward** from its final position, toward the
   display centre. Lower it level to the factory post faces, then slide it
   **10 mm outward** at that height. Do not force it straight down in its final
   lateral position. Both rails use this inward-start, outward-slide path.
6. Start two M2.5 × 6 screws per rail into the factory metal posts, and one
   M3 × 12 screw per raised tower into its captive nut. Seat them gently only
   after the rail is fully located. No rail should clamp the module by bending
   it into position.

The native frame has the display centre at X3, Y0; the factory post-face plane
is Z10.15 mm. These datums describe the CAD installation path, not a substitute
for checking the actual printed fit.

## Rear components and closure

Use the current USB-C tunnel with the current front. The earlier tunnel is not
the matching part. Keep its socket and cable passage clear.

The fixed tray bonds to the rear's central land. Fit the battery with padding,
a soft strap, lead slack and the actual connector orientation. The modeled
envelope is 60.5 × 50.2 × 7.4 mm. Each speaker envelope is 30 × 20 × 4.7 mm;
bond the speaker frames beside the matching rear grilles, keeping glue off the
diaphragms. Keep all wires clear of screws and the closing seam.

Mechanical room does not qualify battery electrical compatibility, connector
polarity or charge current. Check the actual board and pack documentation before
connecting a battery. With the rear open, verify button operation and unobstructed
USB cable insertion. The rear must close freely without compressing the module
or pack. Fasten its four M3 × 12 countersunk screws and fit the cosmetic caps.

## Stands and accessories

The stands are separate passive supports. The landscape stand's exterior front
is continuous; its button clearance is an internal upward-open well. Lower the
pod into the cradle so the housing carries the load. Lift it out if needed to
reach BAT_PWR. The portrait stand is a mechanical orientation option whose
printed fit and firmware layout need their own checks.

Fit ornaments with their recessed/decorative faces toward the screen. Use one
pair in the active orientation and plug the unused sockets. All ornament types
share the keyed stem. Remove brim traces before fitting; start gently and do
not force a tight stem. The small grip ribs may need careful local trimming for
an unusually tight print. The broad stem, housing socket and curved plug face
should not be sanded down merely to remove a brim ridge.

The same pair works in either orientation. Portrait assembly meshes are viewer
poses of those same parts, not extra pieces to print. Holding force, wear and
PLA creep have not been measured.
